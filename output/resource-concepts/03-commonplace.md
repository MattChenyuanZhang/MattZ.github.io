# Concept 03 — Commonplace book

Independent source review of resources.html and stylesheet.css.

## Findings

- The page has no visible page title, intro, or explanation of why Matt selected the links. The sole commented-out annotation demonstrates that editorial context is hidden.
- Section headings are direct children of a single ul rather than section headings followed by their own lists. This loses useful semantic grouping.
- Resources imports Inter but the shared stylesheet requests EB Garamond without loading it. The intended typeface is not reliably rendered.
- Desktop navigation uses a fixed 300px right margin on the name; mobile forces a horizontally scrolling row, making narrow-screen navigation less discoverable.
- Long titles, author homepages, PDFs, archived pages, social posts, and standard articles have effectively the same presentation. Readers cannot quickly identify format or decide where to start.
- No category jump navigation or visited-link visual distinction helps readers navigate or return to the collection.

## Proposal

Make the page a warm personal commonplace book: cream background, dark olive text, sage rules, elegant serif headlines, a compact category index, and short editorial annotations. Keep real resources, separate source names from titles, and label PDFs. Margin notes offer a personal starting point. Collapse the index into an accessible topic chooser on mobile. Featured recommendations and annotation wording in the image are proposed copy for Matt to approve.
